I build machine learning models that infer the physical properties of galaxies from their images, and I care about whether the uncertainties those models report are actually correct.

I'm a PhD candidate in the William H. Miller III Department of Physics and Astronomy at Johns Hopkins, advised by John F. Wu (Space Telescope Science Institute) and co-advised by Timothy Heckman (JHU). Before this I did a BS in physics and electrical engineering and an MEng in EECS at MIT, where I worked on anomaly detection for the CMS experiment at the LHC. I'm interested in research where conditional generative modeling or simulation-based inference is applied to real scientific or engineering problems.

Research

Posterior inference of galaxy properties from images

JHU / STScI · Jan 2023 – present · thesis work

Conditional flow-matching models that produce full posterior distributions over redshift, stellar mass, star formation rate, metallicity, age, and dust attenuation, conditioned on galaxy images and/or photometry.

Two parallel pipelines, SDSS imaging + photometry and DESI spectrophotometry + DECaLS imaging, let me measure how much morphology tightens constraints beyond what colors give. Posteriors are checked with calibration diagnostics and with astrophysics-specific tests, with a focus on the dust–age degeneracy.

Inferring exoplanet atmospheres from spectra

Harvard–Smithsonian CfA · AstroAI Summer Program · Jun – Aug 2026

Part of a Harvard–MIT collaboration on retrieving molecular abundances in exoplanet atmospheres. I built a pipeline that forward-models spectra for millions of synthetic planets, then trains a conditional flow-matching model to estimate posteriors over 21 atmospheric and planetary parameters.

forward modelingflow matchingexoplanets

Generating galaxy images from physical properties

JHU / STScI · 2023 – 2024

The inverse problem: a conditional diffusion model that produces SDSS-like galaxy images given a set of physical properties. Realism was assessed with morphology statistics (Gini/M20, CAS) rather than by eye.

diffusion modelsmorphology metrics

LLM tools for astronomy literature

JHU Center for Language and Speech Processing · JSALT 2024

On an interdisciplinary team building Pathfinder, a retrieval-augmented chatbot that returns the most relevant NASA ADS papers for a query. I built an embedding-distance anomaly detector to catch irrelevant retrievals and a GPT-4-based consensus scorer to measure agreement among the returned papers.

Earlier work

2020 – 2023
Anomaly detection at the LHC · MIT Harris Group, JHU Maksimovic GroupCo-developed QUAK, an unsupervised method that uses a multidimensional loss space to find signal events hidden in quark/gluon jet data. Built a set of normalizing flows (planar, radial, MAF, neural spline) for density estimation on jet variables; applied them to Monte Carlo and 2018 CMS Run 2 data and recovered top quarks at 4σ. This became my MEng thesis.
2018 – 2020
Nanophotonic grating design · MIT Englund GroupDesigned a GaN grating that scatters light into a Gaussian free-space mode with minimal loss, simulated in MEEP, and wrote a simulated-annealing optimizer over the notch spacings.
2017
Neutrinoless double-beta decay · MIT Winslow LabPaul E. Gray UROP researcher. Generated simulated Te-140 and Sr-90 decay images as training data for a CNN separating isotropic from Cherenkov radiation.
